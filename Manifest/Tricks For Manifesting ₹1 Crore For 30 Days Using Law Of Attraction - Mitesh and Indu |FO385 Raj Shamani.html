Big Picture

Podcast host Raj talks with coaches Mitesh and Indu Khatri about the Law of Attraction and money. They claim your feelings, thoughts, beliefs and actions set a "frequency" that attracts results. The episode is for anyone who feels stuck financially. You'll hear their morning routine, a money-frequency test, and a tapping technique. These are the guests' claims and beliefs. The video does not give independent proof.

Section A: The "Science" of the Law of Attraction

Core idea

Mitesh says everything in the universe is energy. Each energy has a frequency, and the same frequencies attract each other. He says your feelings, thoughts, beliefs and actions all have a frequency, so you attract what you vibrate at.

Key points

  • He links this to physics and to the Bhagavad Gita ("as you sow, so you reap").
  • He says a radio tuned to 93.5 can't pick up another station. Likewise, matching frequencies attract.
  • He splits frequency into "positive" and "negative."
  • He uses four "buttons": feelings, thoughts, beliefs and actions (FTBA).
  • He says good friend groups form because frequencies match.
  • Note: these are the guests' claims. The video gives no independent evidence.

Example / Analogy

Two people get $1 million, the same resources and staff. The person with low inner frequency (jealous, insecure) fails. The person with a money mindset and no resources succeeds.

Why it matters

The guests say you must change your inner state first. Resources alone won't do it.

Section B: Basic vs. Advanced Law of Attraction

Core idea

Mitesh says techniques like affirmations, gratitude and routines are "basic." They fail for people whose inner frequency (old wounds) is not aligned. That is "advanced."

Key points

  • He says no "30-second technique" will change your life.
  • The same routine gives instant results to one person and nothing to another.
  • He says the Law of Attraction brings opportunity to your hand. Holding it depends on your own frequency.
  • He says "Law of Attraction only works if you believe" is the biggest lie.

Example / Analogy

Mitesh describes a boy whose mother cried that his father ruined her life, and said never to be like him. The boy grows up with a hidden "my father is a failure, money is hard" belief. He sabotages interviews. A second boy took a course at 16 and forgave his father. His affirmations then worked.

Why it matters

The guests say old family wounds repeat in your money life, with your partner, and with your boss.

Section C: The Daily Routine to Manifest

Core idea

Mitesh gives a simple one-day routine, with the warning that it works best when old wounds are healed.

Key points

  • Morning: say thank you for 10 random things. Don't write a list.
  • Drink "magic water" from a bottle with your life goals written on it.
  • Look at your vision board.
  • During the day, say "cancel, cancel" when a negative word slips out.
  • For a negative event, say the four Hoʻoponopono lines: "I'm sorry, please forgive me, thank you, I love you."
  • Night: read affirmations before sleep, in your own language and words (Indu's "daily magic practice").

Example / Analogy

Indu says that complaining all day cancels the morning routine. Replace "Raj" with "money" in a complaint and ask if you'd go near that person.

Why it matters

What you feel and say all day counts as much as the morning ritual, in their view.

Section D: Your Relationship With Money

Core idea

The coaches say people stay poor because their relationship with money is bad. Money is energy, and how you treat it affects what comes.

Key points

  • Sign 1: your current financial results. Do the same money problems keep repeating?
  • Sign 2: your emotions about money. Bankrupt and feeling bankrupt are different.
  • Sign 3: your beliefs about money.
  • Financial frequency means the amount of money you feel comfortable with.
  • If your income jumps beyond your comfort zone, you may self-sabotage and fall back.
  • They say 90% of people struggling with money have problems that start with their parents.

Example / Analogy

A woman earning about 37 lakh a year cried and felt poor because her last salary was 45 lakh. Also, Mitesh's analogy: being rude to a dinner guest makes them stop coming. Treating money with disrespect does the same.

Why it matters

The guests say feeling poor, not just being poor, shapes your results.

Section E: Money Wounds and Blocks

Core idea

The guests say childhood experiences create hidden "money wounds." Four fingers (FTBA) block money from coming in.

Key points

  • A man always had debts. He realized his father also borrowed from people, was cheated, and felt bad. He copied it.
  • Raj did a money-frequency test live. Raj's answers: uncomfortable asking for money, feeling "compromised" when he can't buy something expensive, and saying no to free money and commissions.
  • Raj's pattern: "I must work hard for money," so he refuses gifts and easy money.
  • Mitesh says "free" and "easy" money feels wrong to people who believe money needs hard work.
  • He says one person's effort can't match a very large goal, so "partner with the universe."
  • Limiting beliefs: "making money is difficult," "free money is bad."
  • Limiting actions: "I'm lazy," "I take no risks."

Example / Analogy

Mitesh says bad breath is hard to notice yourself. Likewise, we can't see our own patterns, but others see them quickly. A printer analogy: Raj believed "I'm a printer, input equals output."

Why it matters

Awareness of the block is step one. Raj said the test itself unlocked something.

Section F: How Belief Is Built

Core idea

Mitesh says you don't need to believe first. Repetition creates belief.

Key points

  • Belief grows from results and repeated action.
  • Repeating a statement can build belief on its own.
  • He gives a negative example: how terrorists are made through repeated messages.
  • Raj's own belief came from his parents, who told him to pick one thing and be number one in the world.
  • The guests say proof from past wins also builds belief.

Example / Analogy

Put "You are a genius" scrolling under a podcast for a year. The child watching it will start to believe it.

Why it matters

You can start before you feel confident.

Section G: Releasing Blocks With EFT Tapping

Core idea

The guests teach Emotional Freedom Technique (EFT, tapping). They say negative emotions get stuck at body "meridian points," and tapping releases them.

Key points

  • Step one: awareness through the test. Step two: release through tapping.
  • Tap on points: side of the hand, eyebrows, sides of eyes, under eyes, under nose, chin, collarbone, under arms, and fingertips.
  • Choose one emotion (for Raj, "I feel compromised when I can't buy something expensive").
  • Recall the moment, find where it sits in the body, rate its intensity from 1 to 10.
  • While tapping, say statements like "even though I can't take this money, I love and accept myself."
  • Later, say positive statements: "I am willing to receive money from every direction."
  • Raj's resistance dropped from about 9 to a lower, mixed level after one round. The guests say five rounds can bring it to zero.
  • Mitesh says awareness and the technique work together, and tapping prevents getting stuck in realization.
  • Note: the guests call this "well established science," but the video offers no evidence for that.

Example / Analogy

A man named Madhu left Infosys, failed 12 interviews, did these exercises, and got a job in seven days, according to Mitesh. The guests give other success stories, such as visas approved after two rejections.

Why it matters

The guests say you should act on awareness rather than sit with it.

Section H: Karma, Hoʻoponopono and Fun Consistency

Core idea

Mitesh says karma and manifestation are the same: actions have frequencies that return to you. He says you can heal old karma, and that consistency works better with fun.

Key points

  • He says energy is shared, so if you hurt someone, their bad feelings reach you.
  • Mitesh compares old karma to a drop of alcohol in healthy food that ferments later.
  • Karma healing: remember the mistake and say the four Hoʻoponopono lines.
  • He says puja can also work, because the person feels guilty and asks forgiveness.
  • He says to go through hard times (like bankruptcy) gracefully, as in the film Anand.
  • Nobody is 100% consistent, so aim for fun, not strict discipline.
  • Indu says people in their 20s often chase material goals when they really want emotional ones, like peace or feeling "good enough."

Example / Analogy

Mitesh says some of his students healed from cancer by going through it "gracefully." This is a claim in the video, with no evidence given.

Why it matters

The guests say healing the past and enjoying the process makes change last.

The guests close with two key points for making money.

Key points

Other people's belief can lift your own.

Step-by-Step Process

A. The daily routine

  1. Say thank you for 10 random things on waking.
  2. Drink your "magic water."
  3. Look at your vision board.
  4. Say "cancel, cancel" for any negative word.
  5. Use the four Hoʻoponopono lines for negative events.
  6. Read affirmations before sleep.

B. Releasing a money block

  1. Take the money-frequency test (answer in 3 seconds).
  2. Spot the emotion or belief that causes a block.
  3. Recall the moment and find where it sits in the body.
  4. Rate it from 1 to 10.
  5. Tap the points while saying release statements.
  6. Say positive "I am willing to receive" statements.
  7. Repeat for several rounds.

Common Mistakes

Warnings

  • Using a quick technique and expecting instant change.
  • Writing a gratitude list instead of saying random thanks.
  • Complaining all day after a good morning routine.
  • Feeling poor even when you have money.
  • Refusing free or easy money.
  • Believing money must always come from hard work.
  • Chasing a material goal when you want an emotional one.
  • Thinking you must be perfectly disciplined.

  2. Childhood money wounds repeat in your adult life.
  3. Notice your money feelings and your comfort zone.
  4. Repetition builds belief.
  5. Release blocks by tapping, and keep it fun.
